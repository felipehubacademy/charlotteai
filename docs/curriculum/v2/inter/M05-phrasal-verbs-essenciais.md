## Unit I01 — Daily phrasal verbs

> **Sub-CEFR**: B1 | **Grammar focus**: Daily routine phrasal verbs (get up, wake up, go out) | **Tense**: CHUNKS
> **Markers**: —
> **Real-life context**: You describe your daily routine using phrasal verbs.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I _____ at 6 every morning."
   **Options**: get up / get on / get out
   **Answer**: get up
   **Explanation**: "Get up" = leave bed. "Get on" = enter (bus). "Get out" = leave.

2. **multiple_choice** — "Please _____ the light when you leave."
   **Options**: turn off / turn on / turn over
   **Answer**: turn off
   **Explanation**: "Turn off" = switch off. "Turn on" = switch on.

3. **word_bank** — "She _____ her shoes before entering."
   **Choices**: takes off / takes out / takes in / takes up
   **Answer**: takes off
   **Explanation**: "Take off" = remove. Opposite of "put on".

4. **word_bank** — "I _____ my coat before going out."
   **Choices**: put on / put off / put up / put down
   **Answer**: put on
   **Explanation**: "Put on" = wear. Opposite of "take off".

5. **fill_gap** — "Please _____ the TV — I want to watch the news."
   **Hint**: switch on
   **Answer**: turn on
   **Explanation**: "Turn on the TV" — common chunk.

6. **fill_gap** — "We _____ for dinner with friends."
   **Hint**: leave home (phrasal "go" + particle)
   **Answer**: went out
   **Explanation**: "Go out" = leave home (often for social activity).

7. **fill_gap** — "He _____ the meeting." (left the meeting)
   **Hint**: phrasal verb "leave"
   **Answer**: left
   **Explanation**: Sometimes simple verbs work too. "Left the meeting".

8. **fix_error** — "I get up early everyday at 6."
   **Hint**: spacing of "every day"
   **Answer**: I get up early every day at 6.
   **Explanation**: "Every day" (two words) is the time phrase. "Everyday" (one word) is an adjective meaning routine.

9. **fix_error** — "She turned on it."
   **Hint**: pronoun goes BETWEEN turn and on (separable)
   **Answer**: She turned it on.
   **Explanation**: With pronouns, separable phrasal verbs split: "turn IT on", not "turn on it".

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "I get up at 6, put on my workout clothes, and go out for a run. When I come back, I turn on the coffee maker."
    **Question**: What does the speaker put on?
    **Answer**: workout clothes
    **Accepts**: clothes
    **Explanation**: The text says "put on my workout clothes".

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"I get up at 6 every day."** — get up
2. **"Please turn off the light."** — turn off
3. **"She takes off her shoes."** — take off
4. **"I put on my coat."** — put on
5. **"We went out for dinner."** — go out

## Unit I02 — Look for, look after

> **Sub-CEFR**: B1 | **Grammar focus**: Phrasal verbs with "look" (look for, look after, look at, look up) | **Tense**: CHUNKS
> **Markers**: —
> **Real-life context**: You use "look" phrasal verbs to describe searching, caring, checking.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I'm _____ my keys."
   **Options**: looking for / looking after / looking at
   **Answer**: looking for
   **Explanation**: "Look for" = search for. "Look after" = take care of. "Look at" = observe.

2. **multiple_choice** — "She _____ her sister's children."
   **Options**: looks after / looks for / looks at
   **Answer**: looks after
   **Explanation**: "Look after" = take care of.

3. **word_bank** — "Please _____ the menu."
   **Choices**: look at / look for / look after / look up
   **Answer**: look at
   **Explanation**: "Look at" = direct attention to.

4. **word_bank** — "I'll _____ that word in the dictionary."
   **Choices**: look up / look at / look for / look after
   **Answer**: look up
   **Explanation**: "Look up" = search information (dictionary, internet).

5. **fill_gap** — "He's _____ a new job."
   **Hint**: searching
   **Answer**: looking for
   **Explanation**: "Looking for a job" — searching.

6. **fill_gap** — "Can you _____ my dog while I'm away?"
   **Hint**: take care of
   **Answer**: look after
   **Explanation**: "Look after my dog" — take care.

7. **fill_gap** — "I _____ the painting for a long time."
   **Hint**: observed
   **Answer**: looked at
   **Explanation**: "Looked at" — past form, observed/admired.

8. **fix_error** — "I'm looking my keys."
   **Hint**: needs preposition "for"
   **Answer**: I'm looking for my keys.
   **Explanation**: "Look FOR" = search. Missing "for" is common error.

9. **fix_error** — "She looks for her grandmother."
   **Hint**: take care = look after
   **Answer**: She looks after her grandmother.
   **Explanation**: For caring, "look after", not "look for".

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "I'm looking for my phone. My sister looks after my kids on weekends. I always look up words I don't know."
    **Question**: Who looks after the kids?
    **Answer**: my sister
    **Accepts**: her sister

## Unit I04 — Separable vs inseparable

> **Sub-CEFR**: B1 | **Grammar focus**: Phrasal verbs — pronoun placement | **Tense**: CHUNKS
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You correctly position pronouns with separable phrasal verbs.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "She turned _____ off."
   **Options**: it / on it / for it
   **Answer**: it
   **Explanation**: With pronoun, separable phrasal verb splits: "turned IT off".

2. **multiple_choice** — "He looks _____ his sister."
   **Options**: after / out / down
   **Answer**: after
   **Explanation**: "Look after" inseparable — pronoun goes after the phrasal: "look after her".

3. **word_bank** — "I picked _____ from the airport."
   **Choices**: him up / up him / for him / up to him
   **Answer**: him up
   **Explanation**: Pronoun between: "picked HIM up".

4. **word_bank** — "She looks after _____."
   **Choices**: them / them all / for them / them up
   **Answer**: them
   **Explanation**: "Look after" inseparable. Pronoun goes AFTER the whole phrasal verb.

5. **fill_gap** — "Please turn the music _____."
   **Hint**: switch off
   **Answer**: off
   **Explanation**: With noun, can separate: "turn the music off" OR "turn off the music". Both correct.

6. **fill_gap** — "Can you pick me _____ at 8?"
   **Hint**: separable particle
   **Answer**: up
   **Explanation**: With pronoun "me", separated: "pick me up".

7. **fill_gap** — "She looks _____ her dog every day."
   **Hint**: takes care of (after)
   **Answer**: after
   **Explanation**: "Look after" inseparable. "After her dog".

8. **fix_error** — "She picked up me."
   **Hint**: pronoun between
   **Answer**: She picked me up.
   **Explanation**: Separable phrasal verb with pronoun: split. "Picked ME up".

9. **fix_error** — "He looks his mother after."
   **Hint**: Inseparable means no split
   **Answer**: He looks after his mother.
   **Explanation**: "Look after" is inseparable — never split, even with pronoun.

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "I turned the TV off. Can you pick me up at 7? She looks after her dad every weekend."
    **Question**: When should they pick the speaker up?
    **Answer**: at 7
    **Accepts**: 7
    **Explanation**: The text says "pick me up at 7".

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"She turned it off."** — separable with pronoun
2. **"He looks after his sister."** — inseparable
3. **"I picked him up."** — separable
4. **"She looks after them."** — inseparable with pronoun
5. **"Please turn the music off."** — separable with noun (either position)

## Unit I06 — Phone English

> **Sub-CEFR**: B1/B2 | **Grammar focus**: Phone English in Inter register — phrasals and expressions for business calls (Fase B extra unit) | **Tense**: CHUNKS
> **Markers**: [survival] [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You handle phone calls professionally — identifying yourself, transferring, taking messages, dealing with bad connections.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — Professional phone identification:
   **Options**: Hello, this is [name]. / Hi, I am [name]. / Hello, my name. / Hi, name [name].
   **Answer**: Hello, this is [name].
   **Explanation**: "Hello, this is [name]" — standard professional phone identification in English. Never "I am [name]" or "my name is [name]" when answering — those work in person but not on the phone. "This is" is the convention.

2. **multiple_choice** — Asking to speak with someone:
   **Options**: Can I speak to [person]? / I want talk to [person]. / Please [person] phone. / [Person] is there?
   **Answer**: Can I speak to [person]?
   **Explanation**: "Can I speak to [person]?" — standard professional request. Slightly more formal: "May I speak to [person]?". "Could I speak to [person]?" also works. All three are polite phone register.

3. **word_bank** — Confirming you are the person being asked for: "Yes, _____."
   **Choices**: speaking / I'm here / it's me / talking
   **Answer**: speaking
   **Explanation**: "Yes, speaking" — concise phone-specific confirmation that you are the person being asked for. Fixed phone convention. "Yes, it's / it is me" works casually but "speaking" is more professional.

4. **word_bank** — Transferring a call: "I'll _____ you through."
   **Choices**: put / pass / get / send
   **Answer**: put
   **Explanation**: "I'll / I will put you through" — phrasal verb meaning "transfer". Fixed phone English. "Put you through to [department]" or just "put you through" (when context is clear). Common in receptionist/secretary speak.

5. **fill_gap** — "She's on _____ line right now. Can I take a message?"
   **Hint**: indefinite article + word for telephone connection
   **Answer**: another
   **Accepts**: the other
   **Explanation**: "She's / She is on another line" / "She's / She is on the other line" — phone English for "she's / she is busy with another call". "Another line" suggests there are multiple; "the other line" implies a specific second line.

6. **fill_gap** — "Sorry, we were _____ off."
   **Hint**: phrasal for connection drop
   **Answer**: cut
   **Explanation**: "We were cut off" — phrasal verb for losing phone connection. Passive voice — the connection cut us off, we didn't do it ourselves. Fixed phrase: "I think we were cut off" / "Sorry, we got cut off".

7. **fill_gap** — "I'll _____ back to you tomorrow."
   **Hint**: phrasal verb for "respond later"
   **Answer**: get
   **Explanation**: "I'll / I will get back to you" — phrasal verb for "I'll / I will respond later" or "I'll / I will follow up". Standard professional phone close when you can't answer immediately. Different from "call you back" (which means literally make another call).

8. **fix_error** — On the phone: "Hello, my name is John."
   **Hint**: On phone — use "This is John"
   **Answer**: Hello, this is John.
   **Explanation**: "My name is" is for face-to-face introductions; on the phone, the convention is "This is [name]". Cross-cultural error: in many languages, the personal pronoun is used; English phone English uses "this".

9. **fix_error** — "Wait a moment, please. I'll pass you."
   **Hint**: "Put you through" is the phone phrasal
   **Answer**: Wait a moment, please. I'll put you through.
   **Explanation**: "Pass you" doesn't mean transfer in English phone register. The fixed phrasal is "put you through". Other phone English phrasals: "hang up" (end call), "hang on" (wait), "call back" (return call).

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "Hello, this is Maria speaking. Can I speak to Pedro, please? Oh, he's on another line. Can you take a message? Please tell him to call me back when he's free. My number is 555-1234. Thanks — sorry, I think we're being cut off. I'll get back to you later. Bye!"
    **Question**: What two phone-specific phrasal verbs appear?
    **Answer**: "cut off" (connection lost) and "get back to" (respond later). Also: "call me back" (return call), "speak to" (talk with).
    **Explanation**: Phone English vocabulary: "this is...speaking" (identification), "speak to" (talk with), "on another line" (busy), "take a message" (record), "call back" (return call), "cut off" (lose connection), "get back to" (respond later). Each is a fixed phone convention.
