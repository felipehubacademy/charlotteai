# Module M02 — What I've done

> **Level**: Inter (B1)
> **Block**: B1 Block
> **Units**: 5 (I01–I05)
> **Theme**: Present perfect — life experiences
> **Module goal**: Student uses present perfect (have/has + past participle) to talk about life experiences with ever/never; knows the most common past participles.
> **Connects to**: M03 (PP vs past simple) — after PP introduction, learn when to use each.

## Module chunks introduced (~30)

- I have / I've + past participle
- She has / She's + past participle
- ever / never
- Have you ever...?
- I've been to... / I've never been to...
- I've seen / I've eaten / I've tried / I've visited
- past participles: been, seen, done, gone, had, made, eaten, drunk, written, spoken
- in my life / so far / before

## Unit I01 — Have you ever?

> **Sub-CEFR**: B1 | **Grammar focus**: Present perfect with ever/never (life experiences) | **Tense**: PRESENT · perfect
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You ask and answer about life experiences (travel, food, events).

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I _____ visited Italy."
   **Options**: have / has / had
   **Answer**: have
   **Explanation**: Present perfect with I/you/we/they uses "have" + past participle. "I have visited" or "I've / I have visited".

2. **multiple_choice** — "She _____ tried sushi before."
   **Options**: has / have / had
   **Answer**: has
   **Explanation**: She/he/it uses "has" + past participle. "She has tried" or "She's / She is tried".

3. **word_bank** — "_____ you ever been to Brazil?"
   **Choices**: Have / Has / Did / Are
   **Answer**: Have
   **Explanation**: Present perfect question with you: "Have you ever been?".

4. **word_bank** — "I have _____ seen that movie."
   **Choices**: never / no / not / nothing
   **Answer**: never
   **Explanation**: "I've / I have never seen" — "never" is the standard negative in PP for life experiences.

5. **fill_gap** — "I _____ never tried that food."
   **Hint**: have/has for I
   **Answer**: have
   **Explanation**: "I have never tried" — present perfect with I.

6. **fill_gap** — "_____ she ever traveled abroad?"
   **Hint**: Have/Has for she
   **Answer**: Has
   **Explanation**: "Has she ever traveled?" — she uses "has" in PP question.

7. **fill_gap** — "We have _____ been to Japan."
   **Hint**: never (life experience negative)
   **Answer**: never
   **Explanation**: "We have never been" — common life experience structure.

8. **fix_error** — "I have visit Italy."
   **Hint**: Need past participle, not base
   **Answer**: I have visited Italy.
   **Explanation**: After "have/has", use PAST PARTICIPLE: "visited" (not "visit").

9. **fix_error** — "She have tried sushi."
   **Hint**: She is singular
   **Answer**: She has tried sushi.
   **Explanation**: She → "has", not "have". 3rd person uses "has" in PP.

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "I have visited five countries: Italy, France, Japan, Brazil, and Argentina. I've never been to Australia. My sister has been to Australia twice."
    **Question**: How many countries has the speaker visited?
    **Answer**: five
    **Explanation**: The text says "I have visited five countries".

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"I've visited Italy."** — own life experience
2. **"She has tried sushi."** — 3rd person experience
3. **"Have you ever been to Brazil?"** — question
4. **"I have never seen that movie."** — never negative
5. **"We have never been to Japan."** — plural negative

## Unit I02 — I've been to many places

> **Sub-CEFR**: B1 | **Grammar focus**: Past participles — common irregulars | **Tense**: PRESENT · perfect
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You describe travel and life experiences using common irregular past participles.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I have _____ to many countries."
   **Options**: been / gone / went
   **Answer**: been
   **Explanation**: "Been" = visited and returned. "Gone" = went and still there. For experiences (where you've visited), use "been".

2. **multiple_choice** — "She has _____ a great book."
   **Options**: read / readed / red
   **Answer**: read
   **Explanation**: "Read" past participle = "read" (same spelling, /red/ pronunciation).

3. **word_bank** — "We have _____ pizza for lunch."
   **Choices**: eaten / ate / eated / eating
   **Answer**: eaten
   **Explanation**: "Eat" → "ate" → "eaten" (past participle).

4. **word_bank** — "He has _____ many languages."
   **Choices**: spoken / speak / speaked / spoke
   **Answer**: spoken
   **Explanation**: "Speak" → "spoke" → "spoken".

5. **fill_gap** — "I have _____ a great movie."
   **Hint**: past participle of "see"
   **Answer**: seen
   **Explanation**: "See" → "saw" → "seen" (past participle).

6. **fill_gap** — "She has _____ a new book."
   **Hint**: past participle of "write"
   **Answer**: written
   **Explanation**: "Write" → "wrote" → "written".

7. **fill_gap** — "We have _____ a lot of work."
   **Hint**: past participle of "do"
   **Answer**: done
   **Explanation**: "Do" → "did" → "done".

8. **fix_error** — "I have went to Italy."
   **Hint**: Use "been" for experiences, not "went"
   **Answer**: I have been to Italy.
   **Explanation**: For experiences = "been". "Gone" = still there. "Went" is past simple (without have).

9. **fix_error** — "She has ate sushi."
   **Hint**: past participle of "eat"
   **Answer**: She has eaten sushi.
   **Explanation**: After has/have, past participle: "eaten", not "ate".

    **Question**: What has the friend done?

## Unit I03 — Just, already, yet

> **Sub-CEFR**: B1 | **Grammar focus**: just / already / yet with present perfect | **Tense**: PRESENT · perfect
> **Markers**: —
> **Real-life context**: You talk about recently completed actions and pending tasks.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I've _____ finished my homework."
   **Options**: just / yet / since
   **Answer**: just
   **Explanation**: "Just" = a moment ago, recently. Goes between have and past participle.

2. **multiple_choice** — "She has _____ eaten breakfast."
   **Options**: already / yet / since
   **Answer**: already
   **Explanation**: "Already" = sooner than expected. Between has and past participle.

3. **word_bank** — "Have you finished _____?"
   **Choices**: yet / just / already / never
   **Answer**: yet
   **Explanation**: "Yet" goes at end of QUESTION or NEGATIVE: "Have you finished yet?".

4. **word_bank** — "I haven't seen that movie _____."
   **Choices**: yet / just / already / since
   **Answer**: yet
   **Explanation**: "Yet" at end of negative sentence: "I haven't / have not seen it yet" = ainda não.

5. **fill_gap** — "She has _____ left the office."
   **Hint**: a moment ago
   **Answer**: just
   **Explanation**: "Just" — recent action. "She has just left".

6. **fill_gap** — "I've _____ done my homework — finished an hour ago."
   **Hint**: sooner than expected
   **Answer**: already
   **Explanation**: "Already" = before now (sooner). "I've / I have already done".

7. **fill_gap** — "Have they arrived _____?"
   **Hint**: question with yet
   **Answer**: yet
   **Explanation**: "Have they arrived yet?" — checking if it has happened.

8. **fix_error** — "I've yet seen that movie."
   **Hint**: yet goes at end, not middle
   **Answer**: I haven't seen that movie yet.
   **Explanation**: "Yet" goes at END of negative/question, not between have/has and verb. Used with negative.

9. **fix_error** — "She has just leave."
   **Hint**: after just, need past participle
   **Answer**: She has just left.
   **Explanation**: After "just", use past participle: "left" (not "leave").

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "I've just finished my report. I've already eaten lunch. I haven't called my mom yet — I'll do it soon."
    **Question**: What hasn't the speaker done yet?
    **Answer**: called my mom
    **Explanation**: The text says "I haven't / have not called my mom yet".

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"I've just finished."** — just (recent)
2. **"She has already eaten."** — already (sooner)
3. **"Have you finished yet?"** — yet (question)
4. **"I haven't seen it yet."** — yet (negative)
5. **"She has just left the office."** — just + 3rd person
