## Unit A01 — Apologise for, accuse of

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Reporting verbs that take "+ preposition + -ing" (apologise for, accuse of, congratulate on, blame for) | **Tense**: REPORTED SPEECH
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You report blame, apology, praise — choosing the verb whose pattern fits.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "She apologised _____ late."
   **Options**: for being / to be / about being / of being
   **Answer**: for being
   **Explanation**: "Apologise for + -ing". The preposition "for" is fixed with this verb. Followed by gerund, not infinitive. "Apologise to be late" is ungrammatical — common L2 trap.

2. **multiple_choice** — "He accused her _____ the email."
   **Options**: of leaking / to leak / for leaking / about leak
   **Answer**: of leaking
   **Explanation**: "Accuse + person + of + -ing". The preposition "of" is mandatory. Without "of" or with wrong preposition, the verb pattern breaks.

3. **fill_gap** — "They congratulated him _____ his promotion."
   **Hint**: preposition + noun/-ing
   **Answer**: on
   **Explanation**: "Congratulate + person + on + noun/-ing". Fixed pattern: on getting the job, on the promotion, on becoming a parent. Not "for", not "about".

4. **fix_error** — "She blamed him to break the vase."
   **Hint**: "Blame + person + for + -ing"
   **Answer**: She blamed him for breaking the vase.
   **Explanation**: "Blame" takes "for + -ing", not "to + infinitive". The full pattern is "blame + someone + for + -ing/noun". Common trap: defaulting to "to + infinitive" with all reporting verbs.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The mediation broke down twice. He accused her of stalling the negotiations. She accused him of moving the goalposts. Both refused to apologise for what the other called bad faith. Only when a neutral third party congratulated each side on small concessions did the conversation resume."
   **Question**: What action by the third party preceded the resumption of the conversation?
   **Answer**: congratulated each side on small concessions
   **Accepts**: congratulated each side / congratulated them on small concessions / congratulating each side on small concessions
   **Explanation**: Three reporting verb patterns layered: "accused her of stalling", "accused him of moving", "congratulated each side on... concessions". Each verb requires its specific preposition + -ing/noun.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"She apologised for being late."** — apologise for + -ing
2. **"He accused her of lying."** — accuse of + -ing
3. **"They congratulated him on the new job."** — congratulate on
4. **"I blame him for the delay."** — blame for + noun
5. **"She thanked me for helping."** — thank for + -ing

## Unit A02 — Admit, deny, suggest

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Reporting verbs that take "+ -ing" directly (admit, deny, suggest, recommend, mind, regret, consider) | **Tense**: REPORTED SPEECH
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You report admissions, denials, and suggestions with verbs that resist "to + infinitive".

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "He admitted _____ the keys."
   **Options**: losing / to lose / lost / having lost
   **Answer**: losing
   **Explanation**: "Admit + -ing" (or "admit to + -ing", but "to" optional in modern usage). "Admit losing" or "admit having lost" both work — never "admit to lose".

2. **multiple_choice** — "She denied _____ anything wrong."
   **Options**: doing / to do / did / done
   **Answer**: doing
   **Explanation**: "Deny + -ing" — no preposition, no "to + infinitive". "Denied doing" or "denied having done" both work for the past meaning.

3. **fill_gap** — "I suggest _____ the meeting until next week."
   **Hint**: -ing form of "postpone"
   **Answer**: postponing
   **Explanation**: "Suggest + -ing" is the most natural form. Alternative: "suggest that we postpone..." (mandative subjunctive from M04). Both work; "+ -ing" is more compact.

4. **fix_error** — "She denied to take the money."
   **Hint**: "Deny" takes -ing, not "to + infinitive"
   **Answer**: She denied taking the money.
   **Explanation**: "Deny + -ing" — fixed pattern. "Deny to take" is a common L2 error. Memorise the family: admit, deny, suggest, recommend, mind, regret, consider — all take -ing directly.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "Under questioning, the witness's posture shifted. He admitted being at the scene that night. He denied knowing the victim personally. He suggested checking the security footage himself. By the end of the hour, only one of those three claims had survived the lawyer's gentle, surgical doubts."
   **Question**: What did the witness deny?
   **Answer**: knowing the victim personally
   **Explanation**: Three "+ -ing" reporting verbs: "admitted being", "denied knowing", "suggested checking". All take direct gerund — no preposition, no "to + infinitive".

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"He admitted losing the keys."** — admit + -ing
2. **"She denied taking it."** — deny + -ing
3. **"I suggest postponing the meeting."** — suggest + -ing

## Unit A03 — Promise, refuse, offer

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Reporting verbs that take "+ to + infinitive" (promise, refuse, offer, threaten, agree, decide, manage, pretend) | **Tense**: REPORTED SPEECH
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You report commitments, refusals, and offers with verbs that take "to + infinitive" rather than "+ -ing".

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "He promised _____ on time."
   **Options**: to arrive / arriving / arrive / for arriving
   **Answer**: to arrive
   **Explanation**: "Promise + to + infinitive". Fixed pattern. "Promised arriving" or "promised for arriving" — both wrong. "Promise" joins offer, refuse, agree, decide, manage as "to + infinitive" verbs.

2. **multiple_choice** — "She refused _____ the question."
   **Options**: to answer / answering / answer / for answering
   **Answer**: to answer
   **Explanation**: "Refuse + to + infinitive". "Refuse answering" is ungrammatical. The to-infinitive carries the refused action.

3. **fill_gap** — "They offered _____ him to the station."
   **Hint**: "to + base form of drive"
   **Answer**: to drive
   **Explanation**: "Offer + to + infinitive" — the offered action is in infinitive form. Same pattern: "offered to help, offered to pay, offered to wait".

4. **fix_error** — "He threatened leaving the team."
   **Hint**: "Threaten + to + infinitive"
   **Answer**: He threatened to leave the team.
   **Explanation**: "Threaten + to + infinitive" — fixed pattern. "Threatened leaving" is wrong despite the gerund's surface plausibility. C1 precision lives in these distinctions.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "She refused to accept the apology. He agreed to drop the issue but offered to revisit it after a week. By the time the week passed, neither had threatened to escalate further — and both had managed to find more important things to argue about."
   **Question**: What did he offer to do?
   **Answer**: revisit it after a week
   **Accepts**: revisit the issue after a week
   **Explanation**: Four verbs all "+ to + infinitive": "refused to accept", "agreed to drop", "offered to revisit", "threatened to escalate", "managed to find". Same family — fluent reporting demands recognising this group.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"He promised to arrive on time."** — promise + to
2. **"She refused to answer."** — refuse + to
3. **"They offered to help."** — offer + to

## Unit A04 — Warn against, talk into

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Complex reporting verbs (warn + person + against/about/not to, persuade/talk into + -ing, discourage from + -ing) | **Tense**: REPORTED SPEECH
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You report warnings, persuasion, prevention with their specific syntactic patterns.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "She warned me _____ the icy roads."
   **Options**: about / for / of / against
   **Answer**: about
   **Explanation**: "Warn + person + about + noun" — for warnings about a danger. Alternative: "warn against + -ing" ("warned me against driving"), or "warn + not to + infinitive" ("warned me not to drive"). Three patterns, all common.

2. **multiple_choice** — "He persuaded me _____ the offer."
   **Options**: to accept / into accepting / accepting / for accepting
   **Answer**: to accept
   **Explanation**: "Persuade + person + to + infinitive". "Talk into" uses different pattern: "talked me into accepting" (+ -ing). Distinguish persuade (to + inf) vs talk into (into + -ing).

3. **fill_gap** — "They talked her _____ the meeting."
   **Hint**: preposition + -ing
   **Answer**: into attending (or out of attending — both grammatical, different meaning)
   **Explanation**: "Talk + person + into + -ing" (persuaded to do) or "out of + -ing" (dissuaded from doing). Both prepositional patterns common with "talk".

4. **fix_error** — "He discouraged me to apply."
   **Hint**: "Discourage from + -ing"
   **Answer**: He discouraged me from applying.
   **Explanation**: "Discourage + person + from + -ing" — fixed preposition. Not "to + infinitive". Same family: "prevent + from + -ing", "stop + from + -ing".

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The senior partner had a way with juniors. He warned them against rushing the first draft. He persuaded them to seek out the most experienced editors. He talked one young lawyer out of resigning during a particularly hard week. He discouraged anyone from working past nine at night."
   **Question**: What did he discourage the juniors from doing?
   **Answer**: working past nine at night
   **Explanation**: Four reporting verbs with their patterns: "warned them against + -ing", "persuaded them to + base", "talked one out of + -ing", "discouraged anyone from + -ing". Pattern variety at C1.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"She warned me about the icy roads."** — warn + about
2. **"He warned me not to go alone."** — warn + not to
3. **"They persuaded me to stay."** — persuade + to
4. **"She talked him into applying."** — talk into + -ing

## Unit A05 — According to, allegedly

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Hedged reporting (according to + source, allegedly, supposedly, apparently, reportedly) | **Tense**: REPORTED SPEECH
> **Markers**: —
> **Real-life context**: You report unverified claims with appropriate distance — journalism, gossip-with-care, academic citation.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "_____ the report, sales are down 15%."
   **Options**: According to / In accord with / For / By
   **Answer**: According to
   **Explanation**: "According to + source" — fixed phrase attributing information. "In accord with" exists but means "in agreement with", not source citation.

2. **multiple_choice** — "He _____ resigned this morning."
   **Options**: allegedly / according / supposed / for
   **Answer**: allegedly
   **Explanation**: "Allegedly" — adverb signaling unverified claim, often legal-news context. Placed between subject and verb. Marks the speaker's distance from the assertion.

3. **fill_gap** — "_____ to my sister, the wedding is off."
   **Hint**: phrase introducing source
   **Answer**: According
   **Explanation**: "According to + person" — attribution. "My sister said the wedding is off" is fine too, but "According to my sister" registers slight detachment from the claim.

4. **fix_error** — "According me, the meeting was a waste of time."
   **Hint**: "According to + person", not used for own opinion
   **Answer**: In my opinion, the meeting was a waste of time.
   **Accepts**: I think the meeting was a waste of time.
   **Explanation**: "According to" is for citing OTHERS, not yourself. "According to me" is grammatically possible but stylistically wrong. Use "In my opinion" or "I think" for your own view.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "According to three former employees, the company had been cutting safety corners for years. Supposedly, the most recent inspection was rescheduled twice. The CEO reportedly told an internal meeting that 'aggressive timelines' would have to continue. Apparently, no one in the room pushed back."
   **Question**: What does the speaker report apparently happened in the internal meeting?
   **Answer**: no one pushed back
   **Explanation**: Four hedging devices: "According to three former employees", "Supposedly", "reportedly", "Apparently". All register distance from claim while still relaying it. Investigative journalism leans heavily on this register.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"According to the report, sales are down."** — source attribution
2. **"He allegedly took the money."** — legal-news hedge
3. **"Apparently, she's moving abroad."** — softer hedge
4. **"Supposedly, the meeting was a disaster."** — distance + scepticism
5. **"The CEO reportedly resigned."** — formal news hedge

## Cross-unit consolidation

By the end of M07, the student should:
- Match reporting verbs to their patterns: apologise for + -ing, accuse of + -ing, congratulate on + noun/-ing
- Use admit/deny/suggest + -ing (direct gerund, no preposition)
- Use promise/refuse/offer/threaten/agree + to + infinitive
- Use warn (+ about/not to/against), persuade (+ to), talk (into/out of + -ing), discourage (from + -ing)
- Hedge with "according to + source", allegedly, apparently, supposedly, reportedly
- Avoid "according to me" — use "in my opinion" instead
