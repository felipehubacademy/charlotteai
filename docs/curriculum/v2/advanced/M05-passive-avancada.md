# Module M05 — Advanced passive

> **Level**: Advanced (C1)
> **Block**: C1 Block
> **Units**: 5 (A01–A05)
> **Theme**: Impersonal passive (It is said that...), double passive (He is said to be...), causative have/get, passive with reporting verbs
> **Module goal**: Student handles sophisticated passive structures used in journalism, academic prose, and formal speech.
> **Connects to**: M06 (Cláusulas participiais) — extends passive thinking into participle clauses.

## Module chunks introduced (~22)

- It is said / believed / thought / reported / claimed that...
- It was once thought that...
- He is said to be...
- She is thought to have done...
- They are believed to have left...
- I had my hair cut (causative)
- She got her car repaired
- We need this done by Friday
- Get + past participle (informal causative)
- Have + object + past participle
- It cannot be denied that...
- It must be noted that...

## Unit A01 — It is said that

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Impersonal passive with reporting verbs (It is said/believed/thought/reported/claimed that...) | **Tense**: PASSIVE · impersonal
> **Markers**: [denso] [formal]
> **Real-life context**: You report common belief or unverified claim without naming a source — news writing, academic hedging.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "_____ that exercise improves mental health."
   **Options**: It is believed / They believe it / It believes / Is believing
   **Answer**: It is believed
   **Explanation**: Impersonal passive: "It + is/was + past participle (of reporting verb) + that-clause". The agent is omitted because the source is general or unspecified — common knowledge, expert opinion. Used heavily in news and academic prose.

2. **multiple_choice** — "_____ that the company will file for bankruptcy next week."
   **Options**: It is reported / They report it / It reports / Reports it
   **Answer**: It is reported
   **Explanation**: "It is reported that + clause" hedges the claim — the reporter isn't asserting the bankruptcy, just relaying what's being said. Useful for unverified or attributed claims.

3. **fill_gap** — "_____ that the universe began with a Big Bang."
   **Hint**: "It is" + past participle of "think"
   **Answer**: It is thought
   **Explanation**: "It is thought that..." — present accepted theory or belief. Same pattern as "It is said/believed/considered/known".

4. **fix_error** — "Is said that he resigned yesterday."
   **Hint**: Missing dummy subject "it"
   **Answer**: It is said that he resigned yesterday.
   **Explanation**: English requires "it" as dummy subject in impersonal passive — unlike Portuguese, where "se diz que" omits subject. "Is said that" is ungrammatical in English. Always "It is said that..."

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The investigation continues. It is reported that three witnesses came forward last night. It is believed that the suspect was acting alone. It cannot yet be confirmed that any property was stolen — but it is widely suspected that this is the work of the same group responsible for the previous incidents."
   **Question**: What is widely suspected?
   **Answer**: that this is the work of the same group responsible for previous incidents
   **Explanation**: Three impersonal passives layered ("It is reported... It is believed... It cannot be confirmed... it is widely suspected"). News register at its most cautious.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"It is said that money can't buy happiness."** — proverb attribution
2. **"It is believed that the painting is genuine."** — expert opinion
3. **"It was once thought that the Earth was flat."** — historical belief
4. **"It is reported that talks have collapsed."** — news register
5. **"It cannot be denied that the project failed."** — formal acknowledgment

## Unit A02 — He is said to be

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Double passive — subject + is said/thought/believed + to + infinitive (or perfect/passive infinitive) | **Tense**: PASSIVE · impersonal
> **Markers**: [denso] [formal]
> **Real-life context**: You report a claim about a specific person, fronting them as the subject instead of using "It is said that...".

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "He _____ to be the best surgeon in the country."
   **Options**: is said / says / is being said / has said
   **Answer**: is said
   **Explanation**: Double passive: subject + is said/believed/thought + to + infinitive. "He is said to be..." compresses "It is said that he is..." into one clause. More elegant and common in journalism.

2. **multiple_choice** — "She _____ to have left the country."
   **Options**: is believed / believes / was believing / believed
   **Answer**: is believed
   **Explanation**: "She is believed to have left" = "It is believed that she has left". Perfect infinitive ("to have left") shows the leaving happened in the past, before the reporting.

3. **fill_gap** — "They _____ to be considering a merger."
   **Hint**: "are" + past participle of "think"
   **Answer**: are thought
   **Explanation**: "They are thought to be considering" — continuous infinitive after "to". The subject "they" is reported as currently considering something.

4. **fix_error** — "He is said that he is wealthy."
   **Hint**: Either "It is said that he is wealthy" OR "He is said to be wealthy"
   **Answer**: He is said to be wealthy.
   **Explanation**: Don't mix the impersonal "It is said that" with the double passive "He is said to be". Two distinct structures — never combined. "He is said that he is" is ungrammatical hybrid.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The CEO is said to be furious. She is reported to have demanded a complete review of last quarter's accounts. Two senior auditors are believed to have been dismissed since Monday. The company is thought to be preparing a public statement."
   **Question**: How many auditors are believed to have been dismissed?
   **Answer**: two
   **Accepts**: two senior auditors
   **Explanation**: Four double passives in succession ("is said to be... is reported to have demanded... are believed to have been dismissed... is thought to be preparing"). Journalism-grade compression.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"He is said to be a recluse."** — present trait
2. **"She is believed to have left the country."** — past perfect action
3. **"They are thought to be in talks."** — present continuous
4. **"The painting is reported to be a forgery."** — present judgment
5. **"He is rumoured to have made millions."** — past action with "rumoured"

## Unit A03 — Have something done

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Causative have/get + object + past participle (services others perform for you) | **Tense**: PASSIVE · causative
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You describe having someone else do something for you — haircut, repairs, dry-cleaning, surgery.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I _____ last week."
   **Options**: had my hair cut / cut my hair / had cut my hair / had to cut hair
   **Answer**: had my hair cut
   **Explanation**: Causative pattern: "have + object + past participle". You didn't cut your own hair — you arranged for someone else to. "Had my hair cut" = arranged a haircut. Distinct from "I cut my hair" (did it yourself).

2. **multiple_choice** — "She _____ next week."
   **Options**: is getting her car repaired / is repairing her car / has repaired her car / repairs her car
   **Answer**: is getting her car repaired
   **Explanation**: "Get + object + past participle" = informal causative. Same meaning as "having her car repaired". "Get" is more common in spoken English; "have" slightly more formal but both widely used.

3. **fill_gap** — "We need to _____ this contract _____ by Friday."
   **Hint**: "have/get" / past participle "review"
   **Answer**: have / reviewed
   **Explanation**: "Have this contract reviewed by Friday" — passive causative with deadline. Implies the reviewing will be done by someone else (a lawyer, an editor), arranged by us.

4. **fix_error** — "I cut my hair last week" (you mean someone else cut it).
   **Hint**: To make clear someone else did it, use causative
   **Answer**: I had my hair cut last week.
   **Explanation**: "I cut my hair" implies you did it yourself with scissors at home. To indicate a barber did it, use the causative: "I had/got my hair cut". Crucial precision in C1 communication.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "Before the conference, she had her speech proofread by a friend, got her suit pressed, and had her laptop checked for the third time. None of it stopped the nerves, but at least she could blame everything except her preparation."
   **Question**: What three things did she arrange to be done before the conference?
   **Answer**: speech proofread, suit pressed, laptop checked
   **Explanation**: Three stacked causatives — "had her speech proofread" (have), "got her suit pressed" (get), "had her laptop checked" (have). All passive causatives, all arranged by her but performed by others.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"I had my hair cut yesterday."** — service arranged
2. **"She got her car repaired."** — informal causative
3. **"We're having the kitchen renovated."** — ongoing causative
4. **"I need to have my eyes tested."** — future causative
5. **"He had his portrait painted."** — formal art context

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Get-passive nuance — unintended/negative events, and "have/get + something + done by + agent" | **Tense**: PASSIVE · causative

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I _____ on the train yesterday."
   **Options**: had my wallet stolen / stole my wallet / had stolen my wallet / had my wallet to steal
   **Answer**: had my wallet stolen
   **Explanation**: "Have + object + past participle" can also describe MISFORTUNE — something done TO you against your will. "I had my wallet stolen" = my wallet was stolen (passive misfortune). Same surface structure as causative; context tells you which.

2. **multiple_choice** — "She _____ in the accident."

## Unit A05 — Mixed passive register

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Integration of impersonal passive, double passive, causative, get-passive across registers | **Tense**: PASSIVE
> **Markers**: —
> **Real-life context**: You select the passive form that suits the register — formal news, conversational misfortune, arranged service.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "_____ that climate change will affect food production within a decade."
   **Options**: It is feared / They fear / Is feared / Fearing
   **Answer**: It is feared
   **Explanation**: Impersonal passive — formal hedged claim. "It is feared/predicted/anticipated that..." — academic and journalistic register.

2. **multiple_choice** — "She _____ to be one of the most influential thinkers of her generation."
   **Options**: is considered / considers / has been considering / is being considered
   **Answer**: is considered
   **Explanation**: Double passive: "She is considered to be..." compresses "It is considered that she is...". Common biographical register.

3. **fill_gap** — "I need to _____ my passport _____ before I travel."
   **Hint**: "have" / "renew" past participle
   **Answer**: have / renewed
   **Explanation**: Causative: "Have my passport renewed" — arranging the service. Distinct from "renew my passport" (you doing the renewal yourself, which makes less sense since governments issue them).

4. **fix_error** — "I had stolen my bike last week."
   **Hint**: For misfortune, the structure is "had + object + past participle" (your bike was stolen)
   **Answer**: I had my bike stolen last week.
   **Explanation**: "I had stolen my bike" reads as "I had committed the theft of my bike" (past perfect active). To describe the misfortune of being stolen from, use "I had + object + past participle".

## Cross-unit consolidation

By the end of M05, the student should:
- Form impersonal passive with reporting verbs (It is said/believed/thought/reported/claimed that...)
- Compress to double passive (He is said to be... / She is thought to have done...)
- Use causative "have/get + object + past participle" for arranged services
- Use the same structure for passive misfortune (I had my wallet stolen)
- Distinguish the two readings by context
- Pair causative with "by + agent" when naming who did it
- Recognise these registers in news, biography, and conversation

M06 (Cláusulas participiais) takes the passive form ("done", "having been done") and uses it to compress entire clauses — closing the C1 grammar core.
