# Module M04 — Subjunctive

> **Level**: Advanced (C1)
> **Block**: C1 Block
> **Units**: 5 (A01–A05)
> **Theme**: Mandative subjunctive (suggest/insist/demand that he be), past subjunctive (were across persons), "lest", fossilised subjunctive
> **Module goal**: Student uses base-form subjunctive after triggers, deploys "were" for hypothetical mood, and recognises formal-literary subjunctive in writing.
> **Connects to**: M05 (Passive avançada) — formal register continues.

## Module chunks introduced (~22)

- I suggest that he be / go / take...
- I recommend (that) she leave early
- They demanded that he resign
- It is essential that he be present
- It is important that she go now
- They insist (that) we be on time
- I wish I were... (universal "were")
- If only she were here
- Lest he forget...
- God save the Queen / Long live the king (fossilised)
- Be that as it may
- Come what may
- So be it

## Unit A01 — I suggest that he be

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Mandative subjunctive after verbs (suggest, recommend, propose, demand, insist, require, request, ask) | **Tense**: SUBJUNCTIVE
> **Markers**: [denso] [formal]
> **Real-life context**: You write formal recommendations, motions, or policy that requires base-form verbs regardless of person.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "The board insists that he _____ resign immediately."
   **Options**: resign / resigns / will resign / has resigned
   **Answer**: resign
   **Explanation**: Mandative subjunctive after "insist that" — base form ("resign") regardless of subject. "He resigns" would be plain indicative. The subjunctive registers the demand. Note: in modern British English, "should resign" is often substituted ("insists that he should resign"); American English keeps the bare subjunctive.

2. **multiple_choice** — "I recommend that she _____ for the position."
   **Options**: apply / applies / applied / would apply
   **Answer**: apply
   **Explanation**: "Recommend that + subject + base form". "She applies" would be indicative (no subjunctive force); "apply" carries the recommendation. Same triggers: suggest, propose, demand, request, require, insist, urge.

3. **fill_gap** — "They demanded that the meeting _____ postponed."
   **Hint**: subjunctive base of "be"
   **Answer**: be
   **Explanation**: "Be" is the bare subjunctive of "to be" — invariant across persons. "Was postponed" would be indicative past, weakening the demand. "Be postponed" — formal, mandative.

4. **fix_error** — "The committee suggested that he was more careful with the budget."
   **Hint**: After "suggest that", use base form — not past indicative
   **Answer**: The committee suggested that he be more careful with the budget.
   **Explanation**: "Was" is indicative — it describes how he was, not what's being suggested. Subjunctive "be" carries the suggestion that he should be more careful going forward. Classic C1 trap.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The motion was carefully worded. 'It is recommended that the chair step down at the next meeting, that an interim be appointed, and that the by-laws be reviewed within ninety days.' The bare subjunctives — 'step down', 'be appointed', 'be reviewed' — gave the document its quietly forceful tone."
   **Question**: What does the motion recommend?
   **Answer**: the chair step down, an interim be appointed, the by-laws be reviewed
   **Explanation**: Three mandative subjunctives: "step down" (base form of step), "be appointed", "be reviewed". All triggered by "It is recommended that". Subjunctive layered for institutional formality.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"I suggest that he be more careful."** — formal advice with "be"
2. **"They demand that she resign."** — base form mandate
3. **"It is essential that we act now."** — base form after adjective
4. **"I recommend that you take the train."** — base form recommendation
5. **"The judge ordered that the report be filed by Friday."** — passive subjunctive in formal directive

## Unit A02 — It is essential that

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Subjunctive after impersonal expressions of necessity/importance | **Tense**: SUBJUNCTIVE
> **Markers**: [denso] [formal]
> **Real-life context**: You write academic/business prose where formality demands subjunctive after evaluative adjectives.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "It is essential that the report _____ on time."
   **Options**: be / is / will be / was
   **Answer**: be
   **Explanation**: "It is essential/important/vital/crucial/imperative that + subject + base form". Here "be" (subjunctive of "to be"). Universal across persons. Indicative "is" weakens the necessity.

2. **multiple_choice** — "It is important that she _____ heard."
   **Options**: be / is / would be / has been
   **Answer**: be
   **Explanation**: "It is important that she be heard" — bare subjunctive in passive form ("be + past participle"). Compare with indicative "she is heard" — different mood, different force.

3. **fill_gap** — "It was imperative that the troops _____ before dawn."
   **Hint**: base form "withdraw"
   **Answer**: withdraw
   **Explanation**: "It was imperative that + subject + base form". Note: even when the main clause is past ("was imperative"), the subjunctive in the that-clause stays in base form. No backshift.

4. **fix_error** — "It is necessary that he is on time."
   **Hint**: Necessity triggers subjunctive — base form, not indicative
   **Answer**: It is necessary that he be on time.
   **Explanation**: "Is" is indicative present; "be" is the subjunctive form needed after "It is necessary that". Common slip — speakers default to "is/are" out of habit.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The protocol was unambiguous. 'It is vital that all personnel be briefed before deployment. It is crucial that no detail be omitted from the report. It is imperative that the chain of command be respected at every step.' Procedural language at its most formal."
   **Question**: What three things does the protocol demand?
   **Answer**: all personnel be briefed before deployment, no detail be omitted from the report, the chain of command be respected
   **Explanation**: Three stacked "It is [vital/crucial/imperative] that + subject + be + past participle" — passive subjunctive used for impersonal formality. Procedural writing leans heavily on this register.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"It is essential that we act now."** — necessity + base form
2. **"It is vital that he be present."** — necessity + passive subjunctive
3. **"It is crucial that no one be left behind."** — passive subjunctive with negative
4. **"It is imperative that the protocol be followed."** — formal directive
5. **"It is important that she feel welcome."** — base "feel" not "feels"

## Unit A03 — I wish I were

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Past subjunctive "were" across all persons (wishes, if-clauses, would rather) | **Tense**: SUBJUNCTIVE
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You maintain formal correctness with "were" (not "was") in hypothetical/wish contexts.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I wish I _____ taller."
   **Options**: were / was / would be / had been
   **Answer**: were
   **Explanation**: Past subjunctive uses "were" for ALL persons (I, he, she, it, we, you, they). "I wish I was taller" is heard in casual speech but considered non-standard in formal English. "Were" is the correct subjunctive form.

2. **multiple_choice** — "If she _____ here, she'd know what to do."
   **Options**: were / was / is / would be
   **Answer**: were
   **Explanation**: Second conditional with "she" — formal English keeps "were". Casual speech accepts "was", but the subjunctive proper is "were" universally.

3. **fill_gap** — "I'd rather you _____ smoke in the house."
   **Hint**: past simple negative "smoke"
   **Answer**: didn't
   **Explanation**: "I'd / I would rather + subject + past simple" for preference about another person's behaviour. Strictly speaking, "didn't / did not smoke" is the past subjunctive form here — same surface as past simple, but mood is hypothetical.

4. **fix_error** — "If I was you, I would accept the offer."
   **Hint**: Subjunctive "were" universal in formal "If I _____ you"
   **Answer**: If I were you, I would accept the offer.
   **Explanation**: "If I was you" is non-standard. "If I were you" is the formal correct form across all registers above casual speech. Note: in some informal native speech, "was" persists, but C1 writing demands "were".

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "Stevens watched the rain pool on the gravel drive. 'If only it were summer,' he said, 'and if I were ten years younger, I would walk to the village without a coat. I wish I were the kind of man who didn't notice weather. But I am not, and never was.'"
   **Question**: What three things does Stevens wish?
   **Answer**: that it were summer, that he were ten years younger, that he were the kind of man who didn't notice weather
   **Explanation**: Three stacked subjunctive "were" forms — formal, literary register. Note final "I am not, and never was" — switches back to indicative for present/past fact.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"I wish I were taller."** — universal "were" wish
2. **"If she were here, she'd handle it."** — formal second conditional
3. **"If I were you, I'd stay."** — advice cleft
4. **"I wish it were summer."** — weather wish
5. **"He acts as if he were the boss."** — comparison + subjunctive

## Unit A04 — Lest, So be it

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Subjunctive after "lest" (formal-archaic) and fossilised expressions ("Be that as it may", "Come what may", "So be it", "Suffice it to say") | **Tense**: SUBJUNCTIVE
> **Markers**: [denso] [literary]
> **Real-life context**: You recognise and occasionally deploy literary or archaic subjunctive forms.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "Speak softly, lest you _____ the baby."
   **Options**: wake / wakes / will wake / woke
   **Answer**: wake
   **Explanation**: "Lest" = "in case... should" / "so that... not". Always followed by base-form subjunctive. "Lest you wake the baby" = so that you don't wake the baby. Highly formal-literary.

2. **multiple_choice** — "_____ as it may, the project must move forward."
   **Options**: Be that / It be / Be it / Were that
   **Answer**: Be that
   **Explanation**: "Be that as it may" is a fossilised subjunctive phrase = "regardless of that". Used to acknowledge an objection while overruling it. Note inverted base form "be" at the start — relic of older subjunctive syntax.

3. **fill_gap** — "_____ what may, I will see this through."
   **Hint**: "Come" subjunctive
   **Answer**: Come
   **Explanation**: "Come what may" = whatever happens. Another fossilised subjunctive — base form "come" without subject. Means "let what comes come". Synonyms: "Whatever happens, no matter what".

4. **fix_error** — "Suffice to say I was disappointed."
   **Hint**: Formal version requires "it" — "Suffice IT to say"
   **Answer**: Suffice it to say I was disappointed.
   **Explanation**: "Suffice it to say" — fossilised subjunctive with inverted "it". Loose modern usage often drops "it" ("suffice to say"), but the proper form keeps it. C1 writing prefers the full phrase.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The ambassador chose his words. 'Be that as it may, the agreement stands. Come what may, neither side will retreat from what was signed. Suffice it to say that any party tempted to abandon the treaty would face consequences I shall not detail here.'"
   **Question**: What does the ambassador imply about leaving the treaty?
   **Answer**: there would be undisclosed consequences
   **Accepts**: serious consequences
   **Explanation**: Three fossilised subjunctives layered: "Be that as it may" (concession), "Come what may" (resolve), "Suffice it to say" (understatement). Diplomatic prose at its driest.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"Speak softly, lest you wake the baby."** — formal warning
2. **"Be that as it may, we must proceed."** — concession + resolve
3. **"Come what may, I'll be there."** — unconditional commitment
4. **"Suffice it to say, the project is over."** — understatement
5. **"So be it."** — acceptance of inevitable

## Unit A05 — Mixed subjunctive

> **Markers**: —
> **Real-life context**: You select the subjunctive form that fits the register and intent.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I recommend that the candidate _____ a second interview."
   **Options**: be given / is given / will be given / would be given
   **Answer**: be given
   **Explanation**: Mandative subjunctive after "recommend that" — passive base form "be given". Base form of "to be" + past participle.

2. **multiple_choice** — "If she _____ here, this wouldn't have happened."
   **Options**: had been / was / would be / has been
   **Answer**: had been
   **Explanation**: Third conditional — "had been" (past perfect subjunctive). "If she had been here" maps to a past unreal condition. Note: even with "she", we use "had been" not "would be".

3. **fill_gap** — "It is essential that no exception _____ made."
   **Hint**: passive base "be made"
   **Answer**: be
   **Explanation**: "It is essential that + subject + be + past participle" — formal passive subjunctive. "Be made" — invariant. "Is made" would be indicative.

4. **fix_error** — "I suggested that he was more honest with his team."
   **Hint**: Mandative subjunctive requires base form, not past indicative
   **Answer**: I suggested that he be more honest with his team.
   **Explanation**: "Was" is indicative — it describes how he was, not what was suggested. The mandative trigger "suggested that" demands base form ("be") regardless of tense in the main clause.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The professor closed her lecture with a quiet line: 'I would only ask that you remember three things. That you read what challenges you. That you write what scares you.    **Question**: What three things does she ask students to remember?

## Cross-unit consolidation

By the end of M04, the student should:
- Use mandative subjunctive after suggest, recommend, propose, demand, insist, require, urge ("that he be / go / take")
- Apply subjunctive after impersonal evaluatives ("It is essential/vital/imperative that... be")
- Maintain "were" universally in wishes, "if I were you", and "as if + subject"
- Recognise and deploy fossilised subjunctive (Be that as it may, Come what may, Suffice it to say, So be it)
- Recognise "lest + subjunctive" in formal/literary text
- Choose between bare subjunctive and "should + base" (modern alternative)

M05 (Passive avançada) extends formal register into more sophisticated passive structures — impersonal passive, double passive, get-passive nuance.
