# Module M16 — Word formation

> **Level**: Advanced (C2)
> **Block**: C2 Block
> **Units**: 5 (A01–A05)
> **Theme**: Prefixes (un-, dis-, mis-, pre-, over-, under-, re-), suffixes (-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -able, -ise), compounds (closed/hyphenated/open), conversion (noun ↔ verb), nuanced word families
> **Module goal**: Student decodes and produces new words systematically — using prefixes and suffixes deliberately, recognising compounds, and converting parts of speech with awareness.
> **Connects to**: M17 (Elipse e substituição) — moves from word-level to clause-level economy.

## Module chunks introduced (~30)

- un- / dis- / in- / im- / il- / ir- (negation prefixes)
- mis- (wrongly)
- pre- / post- (time relation)
- over- / under- (degree)
- re- (again, back)
- sub- / super- / hyper- / hypo- (position/intensity)
- anti- / counter- / non- (opposition)
- -tion / -ment / -ness / -ity / -ism (abstract nouns)
- -ship / -hood (states/conditions)
- -able / -ible (capability)
- -ful / -less (presence/absence)
- -ise / -ize / -ify / -en (verb formation)
- -ly / -wise / -ward (adverb formation)
- closed compounds (lighthouse, smartphone)
- hyphenated compounds (well-known, state-of-the-art)
- open compounds (high school, ice cream)
- conversion (email v., google v., friend v., gift v.)

## Unit A01 — Negation prefixes

> **Sub-CEFR**: C2 | **Grammar focus**: Negation prefixes (un-, dis-, in-, im-, il-, ir-, non-) and which root each takes | **Tense**: WORD FORMATION
> **Markers**: [denso] [morphology]
> **Real-life context**: You form negatives reliably — knowing which prefix attaches to which root and avoiding non-words.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — Negation of "responsible":
   **Options**: irresponsible / unresponsible / disresponsible / inresponsible
   **Answer**: irresponsible
   **Explanation**: "Irresponsible" — "ir-" prefix attaches to roots beginning with "r" (irregular, irrelevant, irreversible). The "in-" prefix has phonological allomorphs: il- before "l" (illegal), im- before "p"/"m"/"b" (impossible, immortal, imbalance), ir- before "r" (irregular), in- elsewhere (incorrect).

2. **multiple_choice** — Negation of "agree":
   **Options**: disagree / unagree / inagree / nonagree
   **Answer**: disagree
   **Explanation**: "Disagree" — "dis-" prefix indicates opposition or reversal of action. "Dis-" verbs are usually opposite actions (disprove, dismantle, disconnect). "Un-" tends to attach to adjectives (unhappy, untidy), though it works for some verbs too (undo, unpack).

3. **fill_gap** — Negation: "His ideas are _____ logical." (prefix the word "logical")
   **Hint**: Prefix that attaches to "l-"
   **Answer**: illogical
   **Explanation**: "Illogical" — "il-" attaches to "l-" roots (illegal, illiterate, illogical). The "in-" prefix family's allomorphs follow assimilation: easier to pronounce "il-l" than "in-l".

4. **fix_error** — "His behaviour was unresponsible."
   **Hint**: Correct negation of "responsible" is "irresponsible"
   **Answer**: His behaviour was irresponsible.
   **Explanation**: "Unresponsible" isn't a word. "Responsible" takes "ir-" (root begins with "r"). Many C2 learners default to "un-" because it's the most familiar negation prefix. Lexical memory matters: each word fixes its own negation pattern.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The audit's findings were unflinching. The decisions had been irresponsible, the reporting illegal, and the executive's denials immoral. Several mid-level managers, who had felt powerless to disagree, were now offered immunity in exchange for testimony. The CEO, displaying the unrepentant tone of someone unprepared for consequences, dismissed the report as illegitimate."
   **Question**: How does the passage describe the CEO's reaction?
   **Answer**: he dismissed the report as illegitimate (and showed an unrepentant tone)
   **Explanation**: Eight negation prefixes in three sentences: unflinching (un-), irresponsible (ir-), illegal (il-), immoral (im-), powerless (-less suffix, not prefix but contrast), unrepentant (un-), unprepared (un-), illegitimate (il-). Each prefix correctly chosen by root.

3. **"Her response was immature."** — im- before m

## Unit A02 — Suffixes for nouns

> **Sub-CEFR**: C2 | **Grammar focus**: Noun-forming suffixes (-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ship, -hood, -ism, -ance/-ence) | **Tense**: WORD FORMATION
> **Markers**: [denso] [morphology]
> **Real-life context**: You convert verbs and adjectives into abstract nouns — essential for academic and analytic writing.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — Noun form of "decide":
   **Options**: decision / decidement / deciding / decidance
   **Answer**: decision
   **Explanation**: "Decide" → "decision" (-sion suffix). Common verb-to-noun patterns: -sion (decide → decision), -tion (create → creation), -ment (develop → development), -ance/-ence (perform → performance, prefer → preference). Each verb fixes its own noun form.

2. **multiple_choice** — Noun form of "happy":
   **Options**: happiness / happity / happyhood / happination
   **Answer**: happiness
   **Explanation**: Adjective → noun via "-ness". Pattern: "happy" → "happiness", "kind" → "kindness", "dark" → "darkness". Note: "y" changes to "i" before "-ness". The "-ness" suffix is one of the most productive in English — almost any adjective can take it.

3. **fill_gap** — Abstract noun for "manage": "_____" (the action of managing)
   **Hint**: Verb + -ment
   **Answer**: management
   **Explanation**: "Manage" → "management" via "-ment". The "-ment" suffix produces abstract nouns from verbs (achieve → achievement, develop → development, judge → judgment, agree → agreement). Common in academic and business writing.

4. **fix_error** — "Her honestness was rare."
   **Hint**: "Honest" has an irregular noun form
   **Answer**: Her honesty was rare.
   **Explanation**: "Honest" → "honesty" (not "honestness"). Irregular noun formation. Many adjectives form nouns with "-ty" or "-ity" instead of "-ness": "honest → honesty", "able → ability", "sincere → sincerity". Memorising these patterns is part of C2 mastery.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The institution's annual report read like a study in formal abstraction. 'Development continued across all departments. Recognition of contribution increased measurably. Leadership demonstrated greater accountability. Membership numbers held steady. The relationship between governance and execution improved markedly. The organisation's overall maturity is now beyond question.'"
   **Question**: How many abstract nouns appear in the passage?
   **Answer**: at least eight (development, recognition, contribution, leadership, accountability, membership, relationship, governance, execution, organisation, maturity)
   **Explanation**: The passage is built almost entirely on abstract nouns — typical of corporate/academic register. Suffixes used: -ment, -tion, -ship, -ity, -ance, -ence. Nominal density is a marker of formal English.

## Unit A03 — Suffixes for adjectives and verbs

> **Sub-CEFR**: C2 | **Grammar focus**: Adjective suffixes (-able, -ible, -ful, -less, -ous, -al, -ical, -ish, -ive) and verb suffixes (-ise/-ize, -ify, -en) | **Tense**: WORD FORMATION
> **Markers**: [denso] [morphology]
> **Real-life context**: You convert nouns and verbs into adjectives, or nouns/adjectives into verbs — flexibly.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — Adjective from "rely":
   **Options**: reliable / relyful / relial / relish
   **Answer**: reliable
   **Explanation**: "Rely" → "reliable" (-able suffix, capability). Note spelling: "y" → "i" before "-able". "-able" attaches to verbs to form adjectives meaning "can be X-ed" or "characterised by X". Examples: drinkable, washable, manageable, predictable.

2. **multiple_choice** — Verb from "modern":
   **Options**: modernise / modernate / modernen / modernify
   **Answer**: modernise
   **Explanation**: "Modern" → "modernise/modernize" (-ise/-ize, "to make X"). British "-ise", American "-ize". Pattern: adjective + -ise = verb. Other examples: standardise, prioritise, normalise, customise. Very productive suffix in C2 vocabulary.

3. **fill_gap** — Adjective from "danger": "_____" (full of danger)
   **Hint**: noun + -ous
   **Answer**: dangerous
   **Explanation**: "Danger" → "dangerous" (-ous, "full of, characterised by"). Pattern: noun + -ous = adjective. Examples: famous, mysterious, courageous, generous, glorious. Often Latinate roots.

4. **fix_error** — "The instructions were uncomprehensible."
   **Hint**: "Comprehend" forms "comprehensible" with -ible
   **Answer**: The instructions were incomprehensible.
   **Explanation**: "Comprehend" → "comprehensible" (-ible suffix; Latin roots tend to take -ible while Germanic roots take -able). Negation = "in-" + "comprehensible" = "incomprehensible". Not "uncomprehensible" — wrong prefix and wrong suffix combination.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The architect's brief was unusually demanding. The building had to be beautiful but practical, ambitious but affordable, modern but respectful of its context. It had to be simultaneously accessible, sustainable, and adaptable to uses we could not yet predict. We were asked, in short, to standardise innovation — a paradox if there ever was one."
   **Question**: What paradox does the architect identify?
   **Answer**: standardising innovation
   **Accepts**: to standardise innovation
   **Explanation**: Many derived adjectives: beautiful (-ful), practical (-ical), ambitious (-ous), affordable (-able), respectful (-ful), accessible (-ible), sustainable (-able), adaptable (-able). Verb: standardise (-ise). Word-formation density is C2-typical.

2. **"The product is sustainable."** — -able with environmental sense
3. **"They want to modernise the system."** — -ise verb

## Unit A05 — Conversion

> **Sub-CEFR**: C2 | **Grammar focus**: Conversion (zero derivation) — noun ↔ verb without affix change (email, google, friend, gift, message, text) | **Tense**: WORD FORMATION
> **Markers**: —
> **Real-life context**: You convert nouns to verbs (and vice versa) fluidly — typical of modern English, especially tech and social vocabulary.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — Verb form of the noun "email":
   **Options**: email / emailify / email-ate / send mail
   **Answer**: email
   **Explanation**: Conversion (zero derivation) — using a noun as a verb without changing form. "Email" was originally a noun (electronic mail); now "to email" is fully accepted as a verb. Many modern English verbs come from this process: to google, to text, to message, to friend, to gift.

2. **multiple_choice** — Noun form of the verb "google":
   **Options**: a google search / a googling / a googled / a google
   **Answer**: a google search
   **Explanation**: "Google" became a verb (to google = to search online). The noun form refers to either the company (Google) or — in informal usage — "a google search" or simply "a search". Brand names becoming verbs is a common conversion path in modern English.

3. **fill_gap** — Verb from noun "message": "Did you _____ him yet?"
   **Hint**: Same form as the noun
   **Answer**: message
   **Explanation**: "Message" works as both noun and verb. "Did you message him?" — verb form, identical spelling. Native speakers do this automatically. C2 fluency includes the ease of using such conversions.

4. **fix_error** — "I will sendmessage him later."
   **Hint**: "Message" can stand alone as a verb
   **Answer**: I will message him later.
   **Accepts**: I will send him a message later.
   **Explanation**: "Sendmessage" isn't a word. Either use "message" alone (verb conversion) or "send + him + a message" (noun usage). The modern conversion makes "message" enough on its own.

By the end of M16, the student should:
- Choose the right negation prefix (un-, in-/im-/il-/ir-, dis-, mis-, non-) for each root
- Form abstract nouns from verbs and adjectives (decision, management, happiness, ability, partnership)
- Form adjectives from verbs and nouns (reliable, dangerous, sustainable, comprehensible)
- Form verbs from adjectives or nouns (modernise, simplify, standardise, harden)
- Recognise and produce closed compounds (lighthouse, smartphone), hyphenated compounds (well-known, state-of-the-art before noun), and open compounds (high school, post office)
- Apply the position-dependent hyphenation rule (hyphenated before noun, open after noun)
- Convert nouns to verbs naturally (email, message, google, friend, gift, screenshot)
- Spell irregular forms correctly (honesty not "honestness", ability not "ableness")

M17 (Elipse e substituição) moves from word-level economy to clause-level economy — leaving things out without losing meaning.
