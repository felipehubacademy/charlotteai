## Unit A01 — Mixed-register narrative

> **Sub-CEFR**: C2 | **Grammar focus**: Open narrative drawing on literary craft (M14), cleft sentences (M03), ellipsis (M17), and casual fillers (M21) within a single short passage | **Tense**: INTEGRATION
> **Markers**: [integrative]
> **Real-life context**: You tell a short personal story that breathes — literary where it should breathe, casual where it should land.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — Which sentence uses both a cleft and a literary verb?
   **Options**: It was on a Tuesday in March that the call finally came. / The call came on a Tuesday in March. / On a Tuesday in March the call came. / The call, finally, came on a Tuesday in March.
   **Answer**: It was on a Tuesday in March that the call finally came.
   **Explanation**: It-cleft for emphasis ("It was on a Tuesday in March that..."), plus "finally" lending the sentence weight and patience. Combines M03 (clefts) and M14 (literary verb choice). C2 integration uses these moves together.

2. **multiple_choice** — Which closing sentence works as a literary fragment?
   **Options**: Nothing else to say. / There was nothing else to say. / Nothing else needed saying. / There remained nothing to say.
   **Answer**: Nothing else to say.
   **Accepts**: Nothing else needed saying.
   **Explanation**: Sentence fragment for emphasis — drawing on M14 (literary rhythm). After a long sentence, a fragment lands the moment. Both options work; the fragment ("Nothing else to say.") is sharper, the participle phrase ("Nothing else needed saying.") is more elegant.

3. **fill_gap** — Open the next sentence with a casual filler that fits the register: "I knew what was coming. _____, it still surprised me when it actually arrived."
   **Hint**: Casual filler that signals casual register
   **Answer**: I mean
   **Accepts**: you know / honestly
   **Explanation**: Casual filler drawn from M21 — "I mean" / "you know" / "honestly". Marks the shift from observation to feeling. Native narrative often uses one such filler per paragraph, particularly at moments of emotional pivot.

4. **fix_error** — Sentence too long and dense for narrative rhythm: "She had spent fifteen years working at the company and during that time she had built three teams, launched two products, and somehow managed to keep her sense of humour intact throughout every reorganisation and budget cut."
   **Hint**: Vary rhythm — long sentence + short follow-up or fragment
   **Answer**: She had spent fifteen years working at the company. Three teams. Two products. And somehow — somehow — she had kept her sense of humour intact through every reorganisation.
   **Accepts**: similar rhythm-varied version
   **Explanation**: Long sentence + tricolon fragment + emphatic repetition. Drawn from M14 (rhythm) and M13 (tricolon). Native narrative breaks dense sentences for breath. The fragment "Three teams. Two products." emphasises the scale.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "It was on a Tuesday in March that the call finally came. I had been waiting six weeks, and — I mean, by then I had stopped expecting anything specific. Walking back from the kitchen with a coffee in my hand, I almost missed the ring. The voice on the other end was the one I had hoped for. Funny how the moment you stop waiting is exactly when the wait ends. Nothing else to say."
   **Question**: What literary technique closes the passage?
   **Answer**: a sentence fragment ("Nothing else to say.")
   **Explanation**: The passage integrates: it-cleft opener ("It was on a Tuesday in March that..."), participle clause ("Walking back from the kitchen..."), casual filler ("I mean"), pseudo-cleft reflection ("Funny how..."), fragment close ("Nothing else to say."). Five distinct C1/C2 moves in six sentences.

3. **"I mean, by then I had stopped expecting anything."** — casual filler
4. **"Funny how the wait ends when you stop waiting."** — pseudo-cleft reflection
5. **"Nothing else to say."** — closing fragment

## Unit A02 — Argumentative essay opening

> **Sub-CEFR**: C2 | **Grammar focus**: Open an argument integrating inversion (M02), clefts (M03), academic register (M12), and argument structure (M13) | **Tense**: INTEGRATION
> **Markers**: [integrative]
> **Real-life context**: You write the opening paragraph of a persuasive essay or op-ed — academic register, structural devices for emphasis, careful claim-hedging.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — Which opener combines inversion and an academic register?
   **Options**: Rarely has a single technology divided opinion as sharply as artificial intelligence has in the past decade. / AI has divided opinion in the past decade. / AI has, like, really divided opinion. / Lots of people disagree about AI.
   **Answer**: Rarely has a single technology divided opinion as sharply as artificial intelligence has in the past decade.
   **Explanation**: Fronted "Rarely" forces inversion ("has a single technology divided") — drawn from M02. Academic register throughout (no contractions, formal vocabulary) — drawn from M12. Combines two C2 moves in one sentence.

2. **multiple_choice** — Which sentence opens with a cleft and a hedge?
   **Options**: It is widely believed that the technology will reshape work — though it could be argued that the reshaping has already begun. / The technology reshapes work. Maybe. / People believe the technology will change things. / Work is changing.
   **Answer**: It is widely believed that the technology will reshape work — though it could be argued that the reshaping has already begun.
   **Explanation**: Impersonal passive cleft ("It is widely believed that...") combined with a hedged impersonal opener ("it could be argued that..."). Drawn from M05 (passive), M03 (clefts), and M08 (hedging). C2 academic writing layers these.

3. **fill_gap** — Open with an inverted concession: "_____ has a single regulation produced such fierce debate as the proposed framework now before parliament."
   **Hint**: "Rarely" or "Seldom"
   **Answer**: Rarely
   **Accepts**: Seldom
   **Explanation**: Fronted negative frequency adverb triggers inversion — opener of choice for argumentative emphasis. "Rarely has..." opens with weight, signals importance. M02 inversion in argumentative service.

4. **fix_error** — Argumentative opener too casual: "AI is a big deal. People disagree about it. Some say it'll ruin things, others say it'll save us. What do you think?"
   **Hint**: Rewrite for academic register with at least one structural device
   **Answer**: Rarely has a technology generated as polarised a debate as artificial intelligence has in the past decade. It is increasingly clear, however, that the question is not whether AI will reshape society but how — and on whose terms.
   **Explanation**: Casual opener → academic opener with inversion ("Rarely has..."), formal vocabulary ("polarised debate", "increasingly clear"), and clausal complexity. Reframed argument question ("not whether... but how... and on whose terms"). Heavy integration of M02, M03, M12, M13.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "Rarely has a single technology divided expert opinion as sharply as artificial intelligence has in the past decade. It is widely believed that AI will reshape the nature of work, though it could be argued that the reshaping has already begun. What is genuinely at stake is not whether change is coming, but how the gains will be distributed — and whose voices will shape that distribution. This essay argues for a third path: a regulatory framework that prioritises distributive justice over both reflexive enthusiasm and reflexive caution."
   **Question**: According to the opening, what is genuinely at stake?
   **Answer**: how the gains will be distributed (and whose voices will shape that distribution)
   **Explanation**: The opening integrates: inversion ("Rarely has..."), impersonal passive ("It is widely believed..."), hedged cleft ("it could be argued..."), pseudo-cleft reframe ("What is genuinely at stake is not... but..."), academic thesis statement. Five distinct C2 devices in five sentences. Op-ed opening grade.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"Rarely has a technology divided opinion so sharply."** — inversion opener
2. **"It is widely believed that change is coming."** — impersonal passive
3. **"It could be argued that the change has already begun."** — hedged cleft
4. **"What is at stake is not whether but how."** — pseudo-cleft reframe
5. **"This essay argues for a third path."** — academic thesis

## Unit A03 — Diplomatic conversation

> **Sub-CEFR**: C2 | **Grammar focus**: Concession (M10) + hedging (M08) + register (M11) + pragmatic openers (M21) — disagree firmly while preserving the relationship | **Tense**: INTEGRATION
> **Markers**: [integrative]
> **Real-life context**: You deliver hard feedback or push back on a position without damaging the working relationship — diplomatic register sustained across a full exchange.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — Most diplomatic refusal:
   **Options**: With respect, I'd see it differently — though I take your point about timeline. / You're wrong. / No, that's not right. / I disagree completely.
   **Answer**: With respect, I'd see it differently — though I take your point about timeline.
   **Explanation**: Three diplomatic moves in one sentence: "With respect" (formal hedge), "I'd / I would see it differently" (soft contrast), "I take your point about" (concession). Drawn from M08 (hedging) and M10 (concession markers). Disagreement without damage.

2. **multiple_choice** — Which feedback opener is most face-saving?
   **Options**: Honestly — and I mean this constructively — I'd push back on one specific point. / You're completely wrong. / This is bad. / I don't like it.
   **Answer**: Honestly — and I mean this constructively — I'd push back on one specific point.
   **Explanation**: Pragmatic opener "Honestly" + intentional reassurance "I mean this constructively" + scoped pushback "on one specific point". Drawn from M21 (pragmatic openers) and M11 (register awareness). Preserves face while opening honest critique.

3. **fill_gap** — Diplomatic reframe of a strong position: "_____ that the proposal has merit — but the timing concerns are non-trivial."
   **Hint**: "I can see / Granted / It's true"
