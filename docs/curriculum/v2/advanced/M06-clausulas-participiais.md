# Module M06 — Cláusulas participiais

> **Level**: Advanced (C1)
> **Block**: C1 Block
> **Units**: 5 (A01–A05)
> **Theme**: Participle clauses — present (-ing), past (-ed), perfect (Having + past participle), perfect passive (Having been + past participle), with-clauses
> **Module goal**: Student compresses two clauses into one using participle constructions, controlling subject matching to avoid dangling participles.
> **Connects to**: M07 (Reported speech masterclass) — nuanced reporting needs compression.

## Unit A01 — Walking down the street

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Present participle (-ing) clauses — simultaneous or causal action
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You compress two simultaneous past actions or describe ongoing setting + main action.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "_____ home, I bumped into an old friend."
   **Options**: Walking / Walked / I walked / Walks
   **Answer**: Walking
   **Explanation**: Present participle clause (-ing) describes the second action's setting — simultaneous to the main verb. "Walking home, I bumped into..." = "While I was walking home, I bumped into...". Subject of participle matches subject of main clause (I).

2. **multiple_choice** — "_____ for an hour, I gave up and left."
   **Options**: Waiting / Waited / I waited / To wait
   **Answer**: Waiting
   **Explanation**: "Waiting for an hour" = "after I had been waiting for an hour" or "having waited for an hour". -ing form here suggests duration up to the main action. Compressed narrative.

3. **fill_gap** — "_____ slowly down the corridor, she heard footsteps behind her."
   **Hint**: present participle "walk"
   **Answer**: Walking
   **Explanation**: "Walking slowly... she heard" — simultaneous past actions. Subject "she" of main clause = implied subject of "walking". No dangling.

4. **fix_error** — "Walking down the street, the rain started suddenly."
   **Hint**: Subject of participle must match subject of main clause
   **Answer**: While I was walking down the street, the rain started suddenly. (or: Walking down the street, I felt the rain start suddenly.)
   **Explanation**: As written, the participle "walking" attaches to "the rain" — the rain wasn't walking! Classic dangling participle. Fix by adding a subject clause or rewriting so the participle subject matches the main clause.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "Standing on the balcony at dawn, she watched the city wake. The first taxis appeared. Wandering shopkeepers raised their shutters. Sleeping pigeons stirred on the cathedral spire. She drank her coffee slowly, knowing the morning belonged to no one and to her alone."
   **Question**: How does the passage open the description?
   **Answer**: with a participle clause — "Standing on the balcony at dawn"
   **Explanation**: "Standing on the balcony" is the opening participle clause, setting the scene with simultaneous action. Followed by other -ing forms ("wandering shopkeepers", "sleeping pigeons") used as adjectives. Literary register heavy on participles.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"Walking home, I bumped into an old friend."** — simultaneous
2. **"Sitting by the fire, she read all evening."** — simultaneous setting
3. **"Looking out the window, I saw it was raining."** — simultaneous observation
4. **"Working late, he missed dinner with his family."** — causal participle
5. **"Smiling broadly, she accepted the award."** — manner participle

## Unit A02 — Built in 1900

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Past participle (-ed) clauses — passive compression
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You describe an object/person with its passive past, compressing "X, which was built..." into "Built..., X...".

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "_____ in 1900, the bridge is the city's oldest."
   **Options**: Built / Building / Was built / Have built
   **Answer**: Built
   **Explanation**: Past participle (-ed) clause — passive compression of "The bridge, which was built in 1900, is the city's oldest." The participle "Built" carries the passive meaning. Common in formal description and historical writing.

2. **multiple_choice** — "_____ in São Paulo, she moved to Berlin at twelve."
   **Options**: Born / Birthing / Was born / Birthed
   **Answer**: Born
   **Explanation**: "Born" — past participle of "bear" used passively. "Born in São Paulo" = "Having been born in São Paulo" / "She, who was born in São Paulo, moved...". Biographical opener.

3. **fill_gap** — "_____ by the team in only six weeks, the prototype exceeded expectations."
   **Hint**: past participle "develop"
   **Answer**: Developed
   **Explanation**: "Developed by the team in only six weeks" — passive past participle clause with agent. Subject of clause = "prototype" (which was developed).

4. **fix_error** — "Building in 1900, the bridge is still standing."
   **Hint**: For a passive past meaning, use past participle (-ed/built), not -ing
   **Answer**: Built in 1900, the bridge is still standing.
   **Explanation**: "Building" would mean the bridge was actively building (impossible). For passive past sense, use past participle "Built". Distinguishing -ing (active) vs -ed (passive) is the crux of participle clauses.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "Founded by immigrants and shaped by half a dozen architectural fashions, the neighbourhood has aged without ever quite settling. Lined with chestnut trees and crossed by trams that have outlived their schedules, it remains a place where strangers still nod at each other across narrow streets."
   **Question**: What two passive participle clauses describe the neighbourhood?
   **Answer**: Founded by immigrants, lined with chestnut trees (also acceptable: shaped by half a dozen fashions; crossed by trams)
   **Explanation**: Four passive participles open this passage: "Founded by...", "shaped by...", "Lined with...", "crossed by...". Compressed description heavy with passive past participles — a literary trademark.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"Built in 1900, the bridge still stands."** — historical compression
2. **"Born in Lisbon, she moved at twelve."** — biographical compression
3. **"Surrounded by mountains, the town stays quiet."** — geographic
4. **"Written in three weeks, the novel won every prize."** — fast creation
5. **"Made of recycled materials, the chair is surprisingly sturdy."** — material compression

## Unit A03 — Having finished

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Perfect participle (Having + past participle) — completed action before main action
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You signal that one event was finished before another began — clear temporal sequence in compressed form.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "_____ dinner, we went for a walk."
   **Options**: Having finished / Finishing / Finished / Finish
   **Answer**: Having finished
   **Explanation**: Perfect participle "Having + past participle" — emphasises that the action (finishing dinner) was complete before the main action (going for a walk). "Having finished" = "After we had finished".

2. **multiple_choice** — "_____ the report, she left early."
   **Options**: Having submitted / Submitting / Submitted / Submits
   **Answer**: Having submitted
   **Explanation**: "Having submitted" sequences events clearly — submission complete first, then leaving. "Submitting" would suggest simultaneity (while submitting), which doesn't quite fit.

3. **fill_gap** — "_____ for an hour, I finally gave up and called a cab."
   **Hint**: "Having" + past participle "wait"
   **Answer**: Having waited
   **Explanation**: "Having waited for an hour" — the waiting was completed (or felt completed) before the main action of giving up. Past perfect compressed.

4. **fix_error** — "Finished my work, I went home."
   **Hint**: Use "Having finished" to mark completion before the main action
   **Answer**: Having finished my work, I went home.
   **Explanation**: Bare "Finished my work" reads as past participle adjective ("being in a finished state"), which is awkward here. "Having finished" clearly marks the sequence: first finish, then go.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "Having spent twenty years in the same office, she felt a kind of vertigo on her first morning of retirement. Having earned the right to rest, she didn't quite know what to do with it. Having always defined herself by what she made, she had to learn what she was without making anything at all."
   **Question**: What three things had she done by the time of her retirement morning?
   **Answer**: spent twenty years in the same office, earned the right to rest, always defined herself by what she made
   **Explanation**: Three perfect participle openers ("Having spent... Having earned... Having always defined") layered to compress a whole biography of completed actions into one paragraph. Common in essay and biography.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"Having finished dinner, we went for a walk."** — sequenced casual
2. **"Having submitted the report, she finally relaxed."** — work sequence
3. **"Having waited an hour, I gave up."** — duration + give-up
4. **"Having lived abroad, she sees home differently."** — long experience + result
5. **"Having said that, I still respect his decision."** — discourse marker form

## Unit A04 — Having been told

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Perfect passive participle (Having been + past participle) — completed passive event before main action
> **Markers**: [denso]
> **Real-life context**: You note what was done TO the subject before they themselves acted — passive history as preamble.

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "_____ told the meeting was cancelled, she went home early."
   **Options**: Having been / Being / Having told / Told
   **Answer**: Having been
   **Explanation**: "Having been + past participle" — perfect passive participle. The subject was on the receiving end of the action (she was told), and it happened before the main action (going home). Compresses "After she had been told...".

2. **multiple_choice** — "_____ raised in three different countries, she speaks four languages."
   **Options**: Having been / Being / Have been / Has been
   **Answer**: Having been
   **Explanation**: "Having been raised" — perfect passive participle. The subject was raised (passive) before the present consequence (speaking four languages). Biographical compression.

3. **fill_gap** — "_____ promoted twice in three years, she felt confident asking for a raise."
   **Hint**: "Having been" + past participle
   **Answer**: Having been
   **Explanation**: "Having been promoted twice" — completed passive events earlier, fuelling present confidence. Subject (she) is the recipient of promotion.

4. **fix_error** — "Being told the news, she immediately called her mother."
   **Hint**: For completed action before main, use "Having been told"
   **Answer**: Having been told the news, she immediately called her mother.
   **Explanation**: "Being told" suggests simultaneity (as she was being told). For a completed event BEFORE her call, "Having been told" is clearer. Both grammatical, but C1 precision favours the perfect form here.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "Having been raised by two demanding teachers, he had assumed all parents were like that. Having been corrected at every dinner table, he had grown into someone who corrected gently. Having been loved fiercely if formally, he loved the same way — and was loved back the same way too."
   **Question**: How was he raised, according to the passage?
   **Answer**: by two demanding teachers / corrected at every dinner table / loved fiercely but formally
   **Explanation**: Three "Having been + past participle" openers compress three formative passive experiences into a portrait. Each shaped a present trait.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

1. **"Having been told the news, she sat down."** — passive receipt + reaction

3. **"Having been promoted twice, she was confident."** — earned passive

## Unit A05 — Reduced relatives + with-clauses

> **Sub-CEFR**: C1 | **Grammar focus**: Reduced relative clauses (the man sitting / the book written) + with-clauses (with everyone watching)
> **Markers**: —
> **Real-life context**: You compress relative clauses into participle phrases and add accompanying circumstances with "with + noun + -ing/-ed".

### 1. Grammar (5 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "The man _____ in the corner is my uncle."
   **Options**: sitting / who sits / sit / who is sitting
   **Answer**: sitting
   **Explanation**: Reduced relative clause: "The man sitting in the corner" = "The man who is sitting in the corner". Drop "who is" to compress. Common in descriptive English.

2. **multiple_choice** — "The book _____ by Saramago is on the table."
   **Options**: written / writing / wrote / which writes
   **Answer**: written
   **Explanation**: "Written" — past participle for passive reduced relative. "The book written by Saramago" = "The book that was written by Saramago". Compressed by dropping "that was".

3. **fill_gap** — "_____ everyone watching, she had to stay calm."
   **Hint**: "With" + "everyone watching"
   **Answer**: With
   **Explanation**: "With + noun + -ing" — accompanying circumstance. "With everyone watching" = "while everyone was watching". Adds context that's happening alongside the main action.

4. **fix_error** — "The book wrote by my favourite author is on the table."
   **Hint**: Past participle of "write" = "written"
   **Answer**: The book written by my favourite author is on the table.
   **Explanation**: "Wrote" is the past simple form. For reduced passive relative, you need the past participle "written". Common slip for irregular verbs.

5. **read_answer**
   **Passage**: "The waiter, balancing four plates and wearing the practised smile of someone who'd done this a thousand times, made it through the crowded dining room without spilling a drop. With diners watching, with chefs shouting, with the room nearly full to capacity, he placed each plate down as if the night were quiet."
   **Question**: How does the passage compress the waiter's situation?
   **Answer**: with three "with + noun + -ing" clauses (diners watching, chefs shouting, room nearly full)
   **Explanation**: Two reduced relatives open ("balancing four plates and wearing the practised smile") followed by three with-clauses ("With diners watching, with chefs shouting, with the room nearly full"). Compressed atmospheric writing.

By the end of M06, the student should:
- Open clauses with present participle (-ing) for simultaneous/causal action
- Open clauses with past participle (-ed) for passive description
- Use "Having + past participle" for completed action before main
- Use "Having been + past participle" for completed passive event before main
- Reduce relative clauses: "the man sitting" / "the book written"
- Add accompanying circumstances with "With + noun + -ing/-ed"
- Avoid dangling participles by matching subjects
