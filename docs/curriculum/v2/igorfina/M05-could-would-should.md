# Module M05 — Could, would, should, can, might, have to, would rather

> **Level**: Manual do F#did@ (A1)
> **Block**: A1 Block
> **Units**: 5 (F21–F25)
> **Theme**: Pedir com educação, dizer o que consegue, o que gostaria, o que deveria, o que talvez faça e o que precisa fazer
> **Module goal**: Aluno sai usando "Could you...?", "May I...?", "I can / I can't", "I would love...", "I'd rather...", "You should...", "I might..." e "I have to / she has to" em situações de viagem.
> **Connects to**: M04 (Gírias) e M06 (Comparando).

> Trilha do Igorfina. O tópico F23 é com a imitação do presidente
> (`persona_outfit: igorfina_president`). Escala do Igor: "may" ~50%, "might" ~70%, "sure" 100%.

## Module chunks introduced (~30)

- Could you give me a glass of water?
- Could you take me to...?
- May I go to the restroom?
- May I sit here?
- I can go. / I can't go tonight.
- Can you swim?
- I would love to eat this cake.
- I would love to see the show.
- I'd like a coffee, please.
- I'd rather have a soda.
- There's no place I'd rather be.
- You should watch this again.
- You should try the pizza.
- I might go. (~70%)
- It may rain. (~50%)
- I have to go to Central Park.
- She has to go.
- Do I have to pay?
- I'm sure! (100%)

## Unit F21 — Could you...? May I...?

> **Sub-CEFR**: A1 | **Grammar focus**: Pedido educado com "Could you...?" e permissão com "May I...?" | **Markers**: [qform] | **Tense**: EXPRESSÕES
> **Real-life context**: No restaurante e no táxi você precisa pedir coisas com educação.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "_____ you give me a glass of water, please?"
   **Options**: Could / May / Might
   **Answer**: Could
   **Explanation**: "Could you...?" é o pedido mais educado: "você poderia...?". Dica do Igor: com "could" e "please", ninguém te acha grosso.

2. **multiple_choice** — "_____ I go to the restroom?"
   **Options**: May / Could you / Should
   **Answer**: May
   **Explanation**: "May I...?" pede permissão: "posso...?". "May I go to the restroom?" = posso ir ao banheiro?

3. **word_bank** — "Could you _____ me to Central Park?"
   **Choices**: take / took / taking / takes
   **Answer**: take
   **Explanation**: Depois de "could", verbo básico: "Could you take me to...?" = você poderia me levar até...?

4. **word_bank** — "Excuse me, may I _____ here?"
   **Choices**: sit / sat / sitting / sits
   **Answer**: sit
   **Explanation**: "May I sit here?" = posso sentar aqui? Verbo básico depois de "may".

5. **fill_gap** — "_____ you help me with my bag, please?"
   **Hint**: Você poderia...? (pedido educado)
   **Answer**: Could
   **Accepts**: Can
   **Explanation**: "Could you help me?" é mais educado; "Can you help me?" também funciona.

6. **fill_gap** — "May I _____ a question?"
   **Hint**: Fazer uma pergunta (verbo "ask")
   **Answer**: ask
   **Explanation**: "May I ask a question?" = posso fazer uma pergunta? Em inglês se "pergunta uma pergunta": "ask a question".

7. **fill_gap** — "Could you speak more _____, please?"
   **Hint**: Devagar
   **Answer**: slowly
   **Explanation**: "Could you speak more slowly, please?" é a frase que salva quando o nativo fala rápido.

8. **fix_error** — "Could you to help me?"
   **Hint**: Depois de "could" não vai "to"
   **Answer**: Could you help me?
   **Explanation**: "Could" vai direto no verbo: "Could you help me?".

9. **fix_error** — "I may go to the restroom?"
   **Hint**: Na pergunta, "may" vem antes de "I"
   **Answer**: May I go to the restroom?
   **Explanation**: Pergunta de permissão: "May I...?". A ordem muda como no "Can you...?".

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "Igor: Excuse me, could you give me a glass of water? Waiter: Sure! Igor: And may I sit by the window? Waiter: Of course."
    **Question**: Onde o Igor quer sentar? (responda em inglês)
    **Answer**: By the window
    **Explanation**: "May I sit by the window?" = posso sentar perto da janela? E o garçom respondeu "Of course" (claro).

## Unit F22 — I can, I can't

> **Sub-CEFR**: A1 | **Grammar focus**: Habilidade e possibilidade: I can / I can't / Can you...? | **Markers**: [qform] | **Tense**: PRESENTE · modal can
> **Real-life context**: Um amigo te convida para várias coisas; você diz o que consegue e o que não dá.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I _____ go tonight, I'm free!"
   **Options**: can / can't / cans
   **Answer**: can
   **Explanation**: "I can go" = eu consigo ir / eu posso ir. "Can" não muda com a pessoa: I can, she can, they can.

2. **multiple_choice** — "Sorry, I _____ go tonight. I have to work."
   **Options**: can't / can / cannot to
   **Answer**: can't
   **Explanation**: "I can't go" = não consigo ir / não posso ir. "Can't" é a forma curta de "cannot".

3. **word_bank** — "_____ you swim?"
   **Choices**: Can / Do / Are / Did
   **Answer**: Can
   **Explanation**: Pergunta de habilidade: "Can you swim?" = você sabe nadar?

4. **word_bank** — "She can _____ English very well."
   **Choices**: speak / speaks / speaking / spoke
   **Answer**: speak
   **Explanation**: Depois de "can", verbo básico, mesmo com "she": "She can speak" (nunca "can speaks").

5. **fill_gap** — "A: Can you drive? B: Yes, I _____."
   **Hint**: Resposta curta positiva com "can"
   **Answer**: can
   **Explanation**: Resposta curta: "Yes, I can" / "No, I can't".

6. **fill_gap** — "I _____ find my phone! Can you call it?"
   **Hint**: Não consigo (forma curta)
   **Answer**: can't
   **Accepts**: cannot
   **Explanation**: "I can't find my phone" = não estou achando meu celular. Muito útil em viagem.

7. **fill_gap** — "Can I _____ by card?"
   **Hint**: Pagar
   **Answer**: pay
   **Explanation**: "Can I pay by card?" = posso pagar no cartão? Pergunta essencial no comércio.

8. **fix_error** — "I can to go tonight."
   **Hint**: Depois de "can" não vai "to"
   **Answer**: I can go tonight.
   **Explanation**: "Can" vai direto no verbo, sem "to".

9. **fix_error** — "He cans play the guitar."
   **Hint**: "Can" não ganha "-s"
   **Answer**: He can play the guitar.
   **Explanation**: "Can" é igual para todo mundo: "he can", nunca "he cans".

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "Igor: Can you come to the beach tomorrow? Ana: I can't, I have to work. But I can go on Sunday! Igor: Perfect!"
    **Question**: Quando a Ana pode ir? (responda em inglês)
    **Answer**: On Sunday
    **Explanation**: "I can't" amanhã, mas "I can go on Sunday": no domingo.

## Unit F23 — I would love... I'd rather... (com o presidente)

> **Sub-CEFR**: A1 | **Grammar focus**: Desejo e preferência: I would love to + verbo / I'd like + coisa / I'd rather + verbo | **Markers**: [denso] | **Tense**: EXPRESSÕES · would
> **Real-life context**: Num jantar de gala, o Igorfina imita um presidente exagerado oferecendo tudo "o melhor do mundo". Você diz o que gostaria e o que prefere.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "I would love _____ eat this cake."
   **Options**: to / for / of
   **Answer**: to
   **Explanation**: "I would love to + verbo" = eu adoraria... "I would love to eat this cake" = eu adoraria comer esse bolo.

2. **multiple_choice** — "I'd _____ have a soda, not a beer."
   **Options**: rather / better / prefer to
   **Answer**: rather
   **Explanation**: "I'd rather" = eu prefiro. "I'd rather have a soda" = prefiro um refrigerante. Depois de "rather", verbo básico.

3. **word_bank** — "There's no place I'd rather _____."
   **Choices**: be / to be / being / am
   **Answer**: be
   **Explanation**: "There's no place I'd rather be" = não tem lugar onde eu preferiria estar. Frase de música e de discurso, ótima para elogiar um lugar.

4. **word_bank** — "I'd _____ a coffee, please."
   **Choices**: like / love to / rather to / likes
   **Answer**: like
   **Explanation**: "I'd like + coisa" = eu gostaria de... É o jeito educado de pedir no balcão.

5. **fill_gap** — "I _____ love to see the show tonight!"
   **Hint**: Eu ___ adoraria (forma completa de 'd)
   **Answer**: would
   **Accepts**: 'd
   **Explanation**: "I would love to see the show" (ou "I'd love to"). Desejo educado e animado.

6. **fill_gap** — "Tea or coffee? I'd rather _____ coffee."
   **Hint**: Tomar (verbo "have")
   **Answer**: have
   **Explanation**: "I'd rather have coffee" = prefiro café. Com comida e bebida, use "have".

7. **fill_gap** — "Would you like some cake? Yes, I'd _____ to!"
   **Hint**: Adorar (love)
   **Answer**: love
   **Explanation**: Resposta animada a um convite: "Yes, I'd love to!" = sim, eu adoraria!

8. **fix_error** — "I would love eat this cake."
   **Hint**: Falta o "to" depois de "love"
   **Answer**: I would love to eat this cake.
   **Explanation**: Com "would love", vai "to" antes do verbo: "would love to eat".

9. **fix_error** — "I'd rather to have a soda."
   **Hint**: Depois de "rather" não vai "to"
   **Answer**: I'd rather have a soda.
   **Explanation**: "Would rather" é como "can": vai direto no verbo, sem "to".

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "Igor (as the president): Folks, this cake is tremendous. The best cake. Would you like some? Guest: Yes, I'd love to! But I'd rather have a soda, not champagne."
    **Question**: O que o convidado prefere beber? (responda em inglês)
    **Answer**: A soda
    **Explanation**: "I'd rather have a soda" = prefiro um refrigerante. E aceitou o bolo com "I'd love to!".

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

Igorfina fala no estilo do presidente (imitação). Aluno repete; a pronúncia é avaliada.

1. **"I would love to eat this cake, thank you!"** — aceitar com entusiasmo
2. **"I'd rather have a soda, not a beer."** — dizer o que prefere
3. **"There's no place I'd rather be tonight."** — elogiar o lugar
4. **"I'd like a coffee with milk, please."** — pedir com educação
5. **"Yes, I'd love to see the show!"** — aceitar um convite

## Unit F24 — You should... I might...

> **Sub-CEFR**: A1 | **Grammar focus**: Conselho com "should" e possibilidade com "might" (~70%) e "may" (~50%) | **Markers**: [denso] | **Tense**: EXPRESSÕES · modals
> **Real-life context**: Um amigo te dá dicas da cidade e você diz o que talvez faça.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "You _____ try the pizza in Brooklyn. It's amazing!"
   **Options**: should / might / may
   **Answer**: should
   **Explanation**: "You should" = você deveria. É conselho, dica. "You should try the pizza" = você deveria provar a pizza.

2. **multiple_choice** — "I _____ go to the beach tomorrow. I'm not sure."
   **Options**: might / should / can't
   **Answer**: might
   **Explanation**: "I might go" = talvez eu vá (~70% de chance, na escala do Igor). Usa quando não tem certeza.

3. **word_bank** — "Take an umbrella. It _____ rain today."
   **Choices**: may / should / must / can't
   **Answer**: may
   **Explanation**: "It may rain" = pode ser que chova (~50%). "May" e "might" falam de possibilidade.

4. **word_bank** — "You should _____ this movie again. It's great!"
   **Choices**: watch / watches / watching / to watch
   **Answer**: watch
   **Explanation**: Depois de "should", verbo básico: "You should watch this again".

5. **fill_gap** — "You _____ visit the museum. It's free on Friday!"
   **Hint**: Deveria (conselho)
   **Answer**: should
   **Explanation**: "You should visit the museum" = você deveria visitar o museu. Dica de amigo.

6. **fill_gap** — "I'm tired. I _____ stay at the hotel tonight."
   **Hint**: Talvez (~70%)
   **Answer**: might
   **Accepts**: may
   **Explanation**: "I might stay at the hotel" = talvez eu fique no hotel.

7. **fill_gap** — "A: Are you coming? B: I'm _____! 100%!"
   **Hint**: Certeza total (100%)
   **Answer**: sure
   **Explanation**: Na escala do Igor: "may" ~50%, "might" ~70%, "sure" 100%. "I'm sure!" = com certeza!

8. **fix_error** — "You should to try the pizza."
   **Hint**: Depois de "should" não vai "to"
   **Answer**: You should try the pizza.
   **Explanation**: "Should" vai direto no verbo, igual a "can" e "could".

9. **fix_error** — "I might to go to the party."
   **Hint**: "Might" também não leva "to"
   **Answer**: I might go to the party.
   **Explanation**: "Might" + verbo básico: "I might go".

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "Igor: You should see the sunset at the Brooklyn Bridge. Ana: Good idea! I might go tomorrow. Igor: Take a jacket, it may get cold."
    **Question**: O que o Igor sugeriu levar? (responda em inglês)
    **Answer**: A jacket
    **Explanation**: "Take a jacket, it may get cold" = leve uma jaqueta, pode esfriar. Conselho (should), talvez (might) e possibilidade (may) na mesma conversa.

### 2. Listening/Speaking (5 phrases)

Igorfina fala (voz do Igor). Aluno repete; a pronúncia é avaliada.

1. **"You should try the pizza in Brooklyn."** — dar uma dica
2. **"I might go to the beach tomorrow."** — talvez vá
3. **"Take an umbrella, it may rain today."** — avisar uma possibilidade
4. **"You should watch this movie again!"** — recomendar
5. **"I'm sure, I'm going tonight!"** — certeza total

## Unit F25 — I have to go! (e she has to)

> **Sub-CEFR**: A1 | **Grammar focus**: Obrigação: I have to / she has to / Do I have to...? + revisão do módulo | **Markers**: [review] [qform] | **Tense**: PRESENTE · have to
> **Real-life context**: Você precisa ir embora de um encontro e explica o motivo; depois pergunta se precisa pagar uma taxa.

### 1. Grammar (10 exercises)

1. **multiple_choice** — "Sorry, I _____ to go. My bus leaves at six."
   **Options**: have / has / having
   **Answer**: have
   **Explanation**: "I have to" = eu preciso / eu tenho que. "I have to go" é a frase clássica para sair de uma conversa.

2. **multiple_choice** — "She _____ to go to the airport now."
   **Options**: has / have / haves
   **Answer**: has
   **Explanation**: Com he/she/it, "have" vira "has": "She has to go". Dica do Igor: terceira pessoa, "has".

3. **word_bank** — "Do I _____ to pay for the museum?"
   **Choices**: have / has / having / had
   **Answer**: have
   **Explanation**: "Do I have to pay?" = eu preciso pagar? Pergunta útil em museu, ônibus, praia.

4. **word_bank** — "I have to go to Central Park. Could you _____ me?"
   **Choices**: take / takes / took / taking
   **Answer**: take
   **Explanation**: Frase do curso do Igor: "I have to go to Central Park. Could you take me?". Obrigação + pedido educado.

5. **fill_gap** — "He _____ to work tomorrow, so he can't come."
   **Hint**: Precisa (terceira pessoa)
   **Answer**: has
   **Explanation**: "He has to work" = ele precisa trabalhar. "Has" com he/she/it.

6. **fill_gap** — "You don't _____ to pay. It's free!"
   **Hint**: Precisar (você não precisa)
   **Answer**: have
   **Explanation**: "You don't have to pay" = você não precisa pagar. Atenção: "don't have to" é "não precisa", não é "não pode".

7. **fill_gap** — "Sorry, I have to _____. See you tomorrow!"
   **Hint**: Ir embora
   **Answer**: go
   **Explanation**: "I have to go" = preciso ir. Despedida educada.

8. **fix_error** — "She have to go now."
   **Hint**: Terceira pessoa (she) pede outra forma
   **Answer**: She has to go now.
   **Explanation**: "She has to", nunca "she have to".

9. **fix_error** — "I have go to the hotel."
   **Hint**: Falta o "to" depois de "have"
   **Answer**: I have to go to the hotel.
   **Explanation**: A obrigação é "have to + verbo". Sem o "to", vira outra coisa.

10. **read_answer**
    **Passage**: "Igor: I have to go, my show starts at eight. Ana has to go too, her flight is at ten. Could you call us a taxi?"
    **Question**: Por que a Ana precisa ir? (responda em inglês)
    **Answer**: Her flight is at ten
    **Explanation**: "Her flight is at ten": o voo dela é às dez. Repare: "I have to" com o Igor e "Ana has to" com a Ana.
